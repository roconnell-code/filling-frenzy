One squeeze

Three ways the stop ends.

The meter does not slow down on the way in. It runs, lands on the price, and waits a beat. That beat is the whole game.

Exact

You stay. The price goes up.

Release while the sale matches the number and that amount stays in the till. The next car on the ladder wants more. In Los Angeles, a clean $2.00 is 0.311 gallons and 8.7 miles already in the tank, and you are still at the island.

The stamp

$2.00 goes in the till.

The pump stamps Exact. Sale one is crossed off. Sale two, $5.00, lights up. The canopy till turns from $0.00 to $2.00. Hold again when you are ready. The street price does not change until you drive away.

Over

You lose all the money.

Hold through the beat and the sale clicks to $2.01. The receipt is blunt: over by $0.01, till now $0.00. The miles in the tank do not save the drawer. You are still at the same pump.

Under

You leave with the gas.

Let go early and the stop is over on purpose. This receipt is 0.532 gallons at $6.428, which is 14.9 miles. The till from the exact $2.00 stays in your pocket. The next pump is already named: Pine & 16th, still in Los Angeles, 14.9 miles across town.

After you pull away

The tank is the map.

Miles are dollars ÷ the local price per gallon × 28. At $6.428 a gallon, one dollar is 4.36 miles. At a cheaper pump, the same dollar goes farther. At a European liter price, the sign shows the local currency and the dollar equivalent the car actually uses.

  • A short tank only changes the corner. Harbor & 5th becomes Pine & 16th. Same city, same posted price.
  • A longer tank reaches the farthest real city you can get to. Leave Los Angeles with about 25 miles and the next island is in Orange, at the Orange County price.
  • Keep the streak and the hop can cross a state, then a border. The canopy sign swaps to that place’s regular.

US figures are AAA Fuel Gauge metro and state averages from 30 Sep 2026. Prices outside the US come from OpenVan.camp, CC BY 4.0, and refresh when the game loads.
